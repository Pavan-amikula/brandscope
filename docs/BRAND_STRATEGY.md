# BrandScope — strategy and activation brief

Independent portfolio case study inspired by the supplied P&G Brand Management Summer Internship 2027 description (Stockholm, R000159972). No employment, sponsorship, endorsement or access to P&G systems is implied.

## Executive decision

Prioritize a small, measurable shopper reactivation test over indiscriminate discounting. Valuable inactive shoppers are a **hypothesis-generating audience**, not guaranteed recoverable revenue. Separate the historical gift-retail evidence from a future Swedish consumer-goods application.

Across the full cleaned merchandise dataset, 96 shoppers meet the selected high-value-at-risk rule. Their historical gross purchases total about £428k. This is past spend, not addressable annual sales, current demand, customer lifetime value, or expected campaign impact. The default 2,000-person campaign sandbox is an independent planning scenario and is **not** this 96-person audience. A 96-person audience is generally too small for a modest conversion experiment; the studio should expose that constraint.

## Consumer, shopper and customer distinctions

| Lens | Evidence available | Hypothesis | Validation needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Consumer / product user | Product names and purchases only | Relevant follow-up may outperform blanket discounting | Interviews and category usage research; who uses the product may differ from who purchases it |
| Shopper / purchaser | Order history, recency, frequency, baskets | Past behavior can prioritize a test audience | Consent, identity resolution, channel reachability and controlled outcomes |
| Customer / retailer partner | Country and order-level trading history; many buyers are wholesalers | Retail partners need commercially viable activation | Authorized retailer data, trade terms, availability, contribution margins and feedback |

## Marketing choices

**Audience:** Use segment rules to define and audit eligible shoppers. Exclude nonconsented contacts and identify wholesale accounts before a consumer campaign. This application does not store contact data or send messages.

**Proposition:** Helpful product relevance and a thoughtful reminder. Avoid unsubstantiated superiority, sustainability or performance claims. The dataset cannot establish why shoppers stopped buying.

**Product:** Test a complementary pairing supported by repeated baskets. Association evidence supports an experiment, not an automatic recommendation rollout. Check availability and category suitability.

**Price:** Start with relevance at full price as the comparator to an offer. Include discounts paid on purchases that would have happened anyway. The contribution planner makes this cannibalization visible.

**Place:** CRM is a proposed low-cost test channel if consent and reachability exist. Retail media or social activation requires platform exposure data, budget planning and permission; none is fabricated here.

**Promotion:** Two messages with the same audience, timing and offer; vary one decision at a time where possible. A drafted Swedish line is included to demonstrate localization workflow and requires native review. It does not prove the author's business-language proficiency.

## Measurement and experiment brief

- Business goal: profitable repeat purchase, assessed by incremental contribution per eligible shopper.
- Assignment: eligible shoppers randomized once, 50:50 treatment versus no-message control. Record assignment before exposure; analyze by original assignment.
- Outcome window: predeclare 30 days, with a return-observation policy to avoid early profit claims.
- Primary metric: contribution per eligible shopper, including product, fulfilment, incentive and campaign costs.
- Secondary metric: purchase conversion; the studio provides an approximate conversion power calculation only.
- Guardrails: opt-outs, complaints, returns, data freshness and delivery failure. No numerical safety thresholds are invented as company policy.
- Analysis: uncertainty intervals, randomization integrity, missing outcomes, contamination and wholesale/consumer differences. Use a pilot to estimate contribution variance before sizing the primary metric.
- Scale criterion: positive incremental contribution supported by uncertainty and acceptable agreed guardrails. A positive spreadsheet scenario alone cannot satisfy it.

## Default sandbox worked example

Planning assumptions: 2,000 eligible shoppers; baseline conversion 5%; absolute conversion gain 3 percentage points; pre-discount order value £45; contribution margin 45%; 5% discount on all treatment orders; £600 extra campaign cost.

- Baseline contribution: 2,000 × 0.05 × £45 × 0.45 = £2,025.
- Treatment contribution: 2,000 × 0.08 × £45 × (0.45 − 0.05) − £600 = £2,280.
- Modelled incremental contribution: **£255**, not realized profit.
- Incremental orders: **60**; incremental pre-discount gross revenue: **£2,700**.
- Break-even absolute conversion gain: **2.29 percentage points**.

The available audience check uses an approximate equal-arm conversion test. Monetary outcomes need their own sample-size design. The historical dataset has no campaign exposures and cannot validate these assumed conversion rates or margins.

## English stakeholder summary

“The historical analysis identifies an audience worth investigating, rather than proving demand. I would first validate consent, wholesale status and message relevance. I would then test contribution against a control, including the cost of discounts on baseline purchases. The Swedish sample is too small to justify a national recommendation.”
